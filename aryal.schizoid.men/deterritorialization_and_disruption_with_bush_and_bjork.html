Deterritorialization and disruption with Bush and Bjork

2026-06-17

“To become imperceptible oneself, to have dismantled love in order to become capable of loving. To have dismantled one's self in order finally to be alone and meet the true double at the other end of the line. A clandestine passenger on a motionless voyage. To become like everybody else; but this, precisely, is a becoming only for one who knows how to be nobody, to no longer be anybody. To paint oneself gray on gray.”
― Gilles Deleuze, A Thousand Plateaus: Capitalism and Schizophrenia

It would be quite trivial to conclude that the Oedipal subject finds the femme expression of love and identity in the arts fascinating driven by erotic curiosity, but even then the subject is limited to the flagpole of Freudian analysis. What first struck me about Kate Bush is, a line from her song "Army Dreamers",

like a chicken with a fox, he couldn't win the war with ego

This is what I like to call, an Oedipal classic, a universal one, the mother's utter mockery of her son's inflated sense of pride, the son reduced to a mere weakling, a chicken, and what the son is yet to face, the predatory fox. I knew little about Kate Bush when I first heard this song, I assumed for such a maternal expression, she must have been a mother. But to my surprise, she wrote this song when she was fairly young, and unlike other young poets, say Rimbaud or song writers like David Bowie, this maternal expression of love, from someone who was not a mother, was a sui generis for me. As a teenager, I encountered a form of love which I had no category for. For many years I did not think much of it, but as the years went on, I encountered similar disrupters across different mediums, and this post serves a single purpose, to explain my encounters with Bush, and Bjork and my becoming through difference.

Bjork's Pagan Poetry, a very primal expression of love and eroticism, almost religious, even pagan, and I am all too familiar with Pagan Poetry in the very literal sense, be it the Iliad or the Rg Veda, and their all too patriarchal verses. But frankly Bjork's attempt at ritualistic, pseudo-pagan eroticism is in no way contradictory to my prior understanding of whatever this abstract pagan or ritualistic eroticism could mean however it still remains disruptive. And I want to rest this case with a few lines,

Swirling black lilies totally ripe

This line is repeated many times, adjacent to what pagan priests do with their pagan poetry. Traditionally lilies symbolize purity, associated with the Virgin Mary, and the flower has petals around it's reproductive center, which has inspired many a poet to use it as an erotic device, the disruption here is the inversion of the lily, the symbolism for purity is gone, and now she suggests something repressed, hidden and possibly forbidden. And this is not some uncertain conflict within the speaker, as she follows up with "totally ripe", her desire is absolutely mature and what follows is that this desire can no longer remain dormant.

This verse exposes the subject of her desire:

I find an accurate copy
A blueprint of the pleasure in me

And this is how the song is concluded:

"He makes me want to hand myself over"

The Oedipal subject would reduce this to simply, an expression of forbidden desire, but again there is a disruption, the iconic section of this song, after the main body has concluded, the speaker starts off with repeated "I love him", however the speaker slowly transitions into "She loves him", now by this point both the subject and the object of the affair are now being observed by the very speaker who explained the affair as the first person. The has speaker already expressed that the object of the affair embodies a hidden desire, what you would traditionally think is a surrender to desire, actually shows how the boundaries of the self are dissolved in practice of desire, this was another sui generis disruption for my assembly.

Moving on from language analysis, I want to explore something more personal, it is the eccentricity of these artists, a persona which is the anti-thesis of what I really am, a paranoid and a perceptually anxious machine. This disruption is much more significant for me as the subject is no longer love or desire but rather identity. And for a minute I would like to push aside the art and study just the artist. Both Kate Bush and Bjork stand out in popular culture for their eccentricity, this is tangential to what is generally considered alternative but without the essence of a performance. Now the question of my identity is something I would shy away from addressing but just recently, twitter user noku404 playfully made a remark after misidentifying me with someone else, on the basis that we both give off an "altmaxxing-literatureenthusiast-marxist-autistic energy", this portmanteau of buzzwords is interesting because it attempts to encapsulate an identity, a neurodivergent marxist guy who is really into alternative culture and literature. For me to be perceived as such, it must mean the territory of alternative culture, literature, marxism and neurodivergence is where I was assembled. This notion of being assembled in a territory is quite important, it does not matter if the label placed on me is accurate or not, but what of my assembly made such a description possible in the first place, this simple remark is an identification of a cluster of relations, it is not to be said that I am these labels but that these labels form a social machine. This social machine is now something I will use to explain how the eccentricity of Bjork and Bush disrupts my assembly, my attraction to these artist and their persona is that they seem to escape territories unlike most individuals or even celebrities who have a fixed identity which can be easily labeled with well established concepts. To understand how they escape territories, you could try and label Bjork with art-pop or femininity but none of these could serve as the focal point in understanding Bjork, confronting such identity which escapes all the categories of identity I had in place, was yet another disruption.

Such disrupting encounters are what set these artists as my personal favorite, for they are my becoming.
